Roles and Responsibilities

1 Why roles?

International finance decisions are made by institutions with different objectives. In this simulation, roles create productive internal conflict—your goal is to negotiate a coherent policy to achieve national goals.

Important

Roles are permanent for the simulation. Changes only may occur for significant reasons.

2 Central Banker

Responsibilities:

  • set monetary policy stance (tighten/loosen/hold)
  • manage the exchange-rate regime and reserves
  • maintain credibility and financial stability
  • communicate clearly (policy signals matter)

Common trade-offs:

  • inflation control vs growth
  • defending the currency vs preserving reserves
  • credibility vs flexibility

Typical outputs:

  • short central bank statement (paragraph)
  • policy recommendation to the cabinet

3 Head of Government / Legislature

Responsibilities:

  • set fiscal policy stance (stimulus/austerity/neutral)
  • assess political feasibility and distributional consequences
  • coordinate across roles to produce a unified plan
  • lead crisis decisions

Common trade-offs:

  • political constraints vs economic discipline
  • short-run stabilization vs long-run credibility

Typical outputs:

  • cabinet decision memo (1 page)
  • press statement to the public

4 Finance Minister (Treasury)

Responsibilities:

  • set fiscal policy stance (stimulus/austerity/neutral)
  • manage taxation and government spending priorities
  • assess public debt sustainability and borrowing needs
  • coordinate with the central bank while respecting institutional boundaries
  • prepare budgetary adjustment during crisis periods

Common trade-offs:

  • short-run stimulus vs long-run debt sustainability
  • growth vs fiscal discipline
  • domestic political pressures vs market credibility
  • social spending vs investor confidence
  • fiscal expansion under a fixed exchange rate vs reserve stability

Typical outputs:

  • short fiscal policy memo (1 paragraph)
  • budget stance recommendation to the cabinet
  • debt sustainability assessment (brief justification)

5 Trade / International Representative

Responsibilities:

  • negotiate with other countries (trade, coordination, institutions)
  • represent the country in multilateral forums
  • assess retaliation risks, sanctions, treaty terms, and alignment options

Common trade-offs:

  • national advantage vs system stability
  • hard bargaining vs reputation

Typical outputs:

  • negotiation position sheet
  • proposed agreement text (short)

6 Business and Labor Representative

Responsibilities:

  • represent firms and households affected by policy
  • assess FX and interest-rate exposure (transaction/operating)
  • propose mitigation: hedging, relocation, wage adjustments, support programs

Common trade-offs:

  • competitiveness vs wages and employment
  • openness vs protection

Typical outputs:

  • stakeholder brief
  • support or opposition statement to the cabinet

7 Team governance

A strong team does two things each week: 1) it debates internally (roles disagree), and 2) it submits one coherent country position with a clear rationale.

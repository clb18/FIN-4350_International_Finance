Release Schedule

Important

This page contains the weekly simulation prompts (the “script”). Your instructor may release each week’s section on Canvas and/or direct you to the relevant subsection here.

1 How to use this page

  • Each week includes a student-facing prompt and a submission checklist.
  • Items labeled Instructor-only are guidance for running the round and can be removed from the student site.
Warning

Students: Do not work ahead. Later rounds assume market reactions and constraints that will be announced.

2 Round 1 (Week 3): Country Framework Plan

Chapters: 1–2

2.1 Student-facing brief

Your delegation will submit a baseline plan describing how your country will operate in the international system.

Decide and justify: 1. Exchange-rate regime (fixed, managed, floating) 2. Monetary stance (tight, neutral, accommodative) 3. Fiscal stance (austerity, neutral, stimulus) 4. Trade stance (open, selective protection, high protection) 5. Capital policy (open, managed, controls) 6. Top 3 priorities (e.g., inflation control, employment, growth, credibility)

2.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Country Framework Plan memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form (country-level)
Note

Instructor-only: Evaluate coherence and realism. Use this to establish each country’s initial credibility rating.

3 Round 2 (Week 4): Balance of Payments Stress

Chapter: 3

3.1 Student-facing brief

New data suggest your country’s current account position is becoming politically salient.

Task: Diagnose your BoP situation and propose an adjustment strategy.

Questions to answer: 1. Is your imbalance driven primarily by trade flows or capital flows? 2. What adjustment mechanism will you pursue (policy change, exchange-rate adjustment, demand management, trade measures, or “wait and see”)? 3. What political constraint is most binding?

3.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • BoP Diagnosis + Policy Response memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
Note

Instructor-only: Announce directional outcomes: deficit countries face currency pressure; surplus countries face appreciation or political pressure to expand demand.

4 Round 3 (Week 5): FX Market Pressure and Parity Spillovers

Chapters: 5–6

4.1 Student-facing brief

Global investors are repricing risk. A major economy signals a policy shift, creating interest-rate differentials and FX pressure.

Task: Choose a monetary/FX response consistent with your regime and constraints.

Questions to answer: 1. Do you adjust interest rates? Why? 2. Do you intervene in FX markets? If yes, how do you preserve reserves? 3. How will you communicate the policy (one-paragraph statement)?

4.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Policy memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
  • Central bank statement (short)
Note

Instructor-only: Reward coherence. Punish “fixed peg + no reserves + no rate move” unless paired with controls or assistance.

5 Round 4 (Week 6): Currency Defense vs Regime Adjustment

Chapter: 9

5.1 Student-facing brief

Speculative pressure intensifies. Markets question whether some countries can sustain their current commitments.

Task: Decide whether to defend the currency, adjust the regime, impose controls, or seek coordination.

Questions to answer: 1. What is your red line (inflation, reserves, employment, credibility)? 2. If you defend, what is your exit strategy? 3. If you adjust, how will you preserve credibility?

5.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Policy memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
  • Trade/International position sheet (bullets)
Note

Instructor-only: This round sets up the shock. Record which countries are “fragile,” “stable,” and “appreciation-pressured.”

6 Round 5 (Week 7): SHOCK — Regional War

Chapters activated: 9 and 17

6.1 Student-facing brief

A regional armed conflict escalates and disrupts trade corridors, energy supplies, shipping insurance, and investor confidence. Liquidity tightens and risk premiums rise.

Task: Submit an emergency response package.

Questions to answer: 1. What is your immediate stabilization priority? 2. What do you do about FX policy and reserves? 3. What domestic political pressure is most acute? 4. What international alignment or assistance will you seek?

6.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Emergency response brief (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
Note

Instructor-only: After submissions, release the shock announcement and market reaction. Prepare to initiate mandatory realignment next week.

7 Round 6 (Week 8): Realignment and Successor State Stabilization

Chapters: 17 and 8 (compressed)

7.1 Student-facing brief

Two countries undergo mandatory realignment: - Industralia - Frontieria

Your instructor will assign successor states. Successor states inherit assets and debt asymmetrically and face temporary credibility penalties.

Task: As a successor state, submit a stabilization plan.

Questions to answer: 1. What exchange-rate regime will you adopt now? 2. How will you stabilize expectations given your initial conditions? 3. Do you seek assistance? If yes, what reforms will you offer?

7.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Stabilization plan (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
Note

Instructor-only: Apply the fixed inheritance table and credibility penalties for two rounds.

8 Round 7 (Week 9): Firm-Level Exposure and Domestic Conflict

Chapters: 10–12

8.1 Student-facing brief

Firms and households experience FX-driven losses and rising costs. Business and labor pressure the government for relief.

Task: Propose a policy response that addresses exposure while maintaining external viability.

Questions to answer: 1. What is the primary exposure (transaction, translation, or operating)? 2. Do you subsidize, encourage hedging, protect, or restructure? 3. What is the long-run competitiveness impact?

8.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Policy memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
  • Business/Labor stakeholder brief (bullets)
Note

Instructor-only: Announce distributional consequences: who gains/loses from depreciation and tightening.

9 Round 8 (Week 10): Rebuilding Credibility and Access to Capital

Chapters: 13–14 (combined)

9.1 Student-facing brief

Markets are selectively reopening. Countries must choose between autonomy and conditional access to capital.

Task: Submit a credibility-and-capital strategy.

Questions to answer: 1. What reforms will you implement to reduce borrowing costs? 2. What financing mix do you prefer (domestic vs foreign; short vs long)? 3. What trade-offs are you willing to accept?

9.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Credibility reform package (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
Note

Instructor-only: Provide differential access outcomes: credible reformers gain; inconsistent policies face tighter spreads.

10 Round 9 (Week 11): Tax Competition and Trade Finance Recovery

Chapters: 15–16 (combined)

10.1 Student-facing brief

Countries compete to attract mobile firms and revive trade. Some propose coordination; others pursue aggressive competition.

Task: Choose a tax strategy and a trade finance recovery policy.

Questions to answer: 1. Do you coordinate on taxes or compete? Why? 2. Will you support trade finance (export credit, guarantees)? 3. What are the risks of a race to the bottom?

10.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Policy memo (1–2 pages)
  • Decision Form
  • Trade/International negotiation sheet (bullets)
Note

Instructor-only: Frame this as a prisoner’s-dilemma dynamic; reward credible coordination.

11 Round 10 (Week 12): Designing the New International System

Synthesis (institution-building)

11.1 Student-facing brief

The class will negotiate the principles and institutions of a new international economic framework.

Task: Propose and defend institutional rules for: 1. crisis liquidity and conditionality 2. exchange-rate cooperation (if any) 3. trade and trade finance norms 4. tax and investment coordination principles

11.2 Submission checklist (Canvas)

  • Final country presentation (format specified by instructor)
  • One-page reflection: what theory best explained your outcomes?
Note

Instructor-only: Use this round as the culminating assessment. Emphasize mechanisms and trade-offs rather than “who won.”

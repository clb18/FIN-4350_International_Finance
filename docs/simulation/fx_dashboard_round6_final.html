2 Economic Conditions Index – Final

The Economic Conditions Index tracks each country’s real economic environment.

  • 100 = baseline / normal conditions
  • Above 100 = stronger-than-baseline activity
  • Below 100 = slowing growth / recessionary pressure
Note

This index is separate from the FX Index. A country may have a stable currency while its economy remains weak.

Country Economic Conditions Index Status Notes
Atlantica 98.4 Stabilizing Not strong yet, but recession is no longer deepening rapidly.
Noravia 96.7 Weak growth Strong currency policy came with a larger growth cost.
Pacifica 98.3 Stabilizing One of the clearer signs of improvement from the recession phase.
Industralia 98.0 Early recovery Trade ties and looser policy helped soften the downturn.
Mercatoria 97.8 Early recovery Demand is still weak, but policy is moving in a more supportive direction.
Petrovia 97.2 Weak but stabilizing Commodity conditions still limit growth, though recession pressure eased.
Agrona 97.0 Weak but improving Still one of the weakest economies, but conditions improved modestly.
Frontieria 97.6 Weak but stable Stabilized, though without a strong rebound.
Important

The final outcome is best described as early stabilization, not full recovery.

3 Trade & Alliance Tracker – Final

Trade relationships became more important during the recession phase and into stabilization.
By the final round, countries that expanded or maintained trade linkages were better able to limit the depth of the downturn.

Note

Interpretation: In a recession, trade agreements are less about expansion and more about preserving demand and preventing isolation.

5 Key Insights from the Final Round

  • Trade agreements were more valuable in recession than in expansion
  • Countries with broader trade networks showed:
    • less severe economic decline
    • earlier stabilization
  • Strategic goods agreements helped:
    • commodity exporters (Petrovia, Agrona)
    • but did not fully offset global demand weakness

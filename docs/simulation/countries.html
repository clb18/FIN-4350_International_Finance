Countries

1 Why fictional countries?

All countries in the simulation are fictional. Their backgrounds are based on real historical experiences, but no country is intended to be a direct copy of any modern state. You may see some similarities.

Note

Learning goal: You will reason from initial conditions and constraints, then choose policies. Countries are not scripted to “win” or “lose.”

2 What is pre-set vs. what you choose

2.1 Pre-set (initial conditions)

These are slow-moving fundamentals and the starting macro snapshot:

  1. Economic structure: what your country produces and trades
  2. Institutions & credibility: ability to commit and be believed
  3. Financial depth: access to markets, banking strength
  4. Politics: domestic coalitions (tariff pressure, inflation aversion, etc.)
  5. Starting snapshot: inflation, reserves, external balance, debt, unemployment (in tiers, not precise numbers)

2.2 You choose (policy path)

Starting in Round 1, your delegation chooses the strategic direction of your country. These choices determine how markets interpret your credibility, how capital flows respond, and how vulnerable you are to shocks.

1. Exchange-rate regime (fixed / managed / floating)

What you are choosing:
How your currency’s value is determined and how much control your central bank retains over monetary policy.

  • Fixed:
    • Currency is pegged to another currency or basket.
    • Central bank must use reserves to defend the rate.
    • Lower short-term volatility.
    • Limited monetary independence.
    • Vulnerable if reserves are insufficient during stress.
  • Managed (dirty float):
    • Currency moves with market forces but central bank intervenes occasionally.
    • Partial monetary independence.
    • Moderate reserve use.
    • Can smooth volatility but may create credibility questions.
  • Floating:
    • Currency determined primarily by supply and demand.
    • Full monetary independence.
    • Exchange rate absorbs shocks.
    • Higher short-term volatility but fewer reserve constraints.
  • Most constrained by:
    • External balance
    • Foreign reserves
    • Financial depth
    • Institutional credibility
    • Export concentration

2. Monetary stance (tighten / hold / loosen)

What you are choosing:
The level of interest rates and liquidity in your economy.

  • Tighten:
    • Raise interest rates or reduce money supply.
    • Reduces inflation pressure.
    • Attracts capital inflows.
    • May slow growth and increase unemployment.
  • Hold:
    • Maintain current interest rate settings.
    • Signals stability.
    • Markets evaluate consistency with economic conditions.
  • Loosen:
    • Lower interest rates or expand liquidity.
    • Stimulates growth and employment.
    • May weaken currency and raise inflation risk.
    • May trigger capital outflows if credibility is low.
  • Most constrained by:
    • Inflation level
    • External balance
    • Credibility
    • Financial depth

3. Fiscal stance (austerity / neutral / stimulus)

What you are choosing:
Government spending and taxation policy.

  • Austerity:
    • Reduce deficits (cut spending or raise taxes).
    • Improves debt sustainability and credibility.
    • May slow growth in the short run.
  • Neutral:
    • Maintain current fiscal trajectory.
    • Signals stability but may not address shocks.
  • Stimulus:
    • Increase spending or reduce taxes.
    • Supports growth and employment.
    • May increase debt and inflation expectations.
    • Can widen trade deficits through higher imports.
  • Most constrained by:
    • Public debt level
    • External balance
    • Political coalitions
    • Credibility

4. Trade posture (open / selective protection / high protection)

What you are choosing:
The degree of openness to imports and exports.

  • Open:
    • Low tariffs and minimal barriers.
    • Increases trade volume and efficiency.
    • Exposes domestic industries to competition.
  • Selective protection:
    • Targeted tariffs or quotas in key sectors.
    • Protects strategic industries.
    • May invite retaliation.
  • High protection:
    • Broad tariffs and strong barriers.
    • Shields domestic producers.
    • Raises consumer prices and reduces trade flows.
    • May provoke trade conflict.
  • Most constrained by:
    • Economics structure
    • Export concentration
    • Domestic coalitions
    • External balance

5. Capital policy (open / managed / restricted)

What you are choosing:
How freely money moves across your borders.

  • Open:
    • Free movement of capital in and out.
    • Encourages investment inflows.
    • Increases exposure to sudden stops and capital flight.
  • Managed:
    • Some monitoring or targeted controls.
    • Moderates volatility.
    • Markets may question transparency.
  • Restricted:
    • Strong limits on cross-border financial flows.
    • Reduces volatility from external shocks.
    • May reduce foreign investment and credibility.
  • Most constrained by:
    • Financial depth
    • Credibility
    • External balance
    • Reserve level

6. Crisis response tools and international alignment

What you are choosing:
How willing your country is to coordinate internationally and what stabilization tools you are prepared to use.

Examples include: - Forming trade agreements - Establishing swap lines - Requesting international assistance - Coordinating interest-rate policy - Joining regional blocs - Adjusting exchange-rate commitments

Key trade-off:
Greater coordination can improve stability and credibility, but may reduce policy autonomy.

  • Most constrained by:
    • Credibility
    • Reserve level
    • Domestic politics
    • Financial depth
Important

Agency rule: Your initial snapshot creates constraints, but your policies can change outcomes over time (for better or worse). Markets respond not only to what you choose, but whether your choices are internally consistent and credible over time.

3 Initial countries

Each country below is defined by a Country Dossier. The tier options are: 1. Low / Medium / High 2. Strong / Moderate / Weak 3. Open / Managed / Restricted

3.1 Atlantica (Systemic financial power)

Structural profile - Diversified, service-heavy economy with deep financial markets. - Large role in global finance and safe-asset supply.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: High
  2. Inflation: Low–Medium
  3. External balance (current account): Deficit (Medium)
  4. FX reserves: Medium
  5. Public debt: High
  6. Financial depth: High
  7. Institutional credibility: Strong
  8. Trade openness: High
  9. Capital mobility: Open

Domestic politics - Voters care about growth and jobs; inflation tolerance is limited. - Financial sector has influence; exporters push back against overvaluation.

Main constraints - Policy spillovers matter: your decisions affect everyone. - Persistent deficits create periodic political pressure.

What you control (examples) - Whether to prioritize global stability or domestic goals. - Whether to tighten policy to support credibility vs. ease to support growth.

3.2 Noravia (High-credibility export champion)

Structural profile - High-income, export-led economy with strong institutions and a large tradables sector.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: High
  2. Inflation: Low
  3. External balance (current account): Surplus (High)
  4. FX reserves: High
  5. Public debt: Low-Medium
  6. Financial depth: High
  7. Institutional credibility: Strong
  8. Trade openness: High
  9. Capital mobility: Open

Domestic politics - Export sector wants a competitive currency; households dislike inflation. - Strong preference for rules-based policy and fiscal restraint.

Main constraints - Appreciation pressure during turmoil. - External partners may accuse you of “excess surpluses.”

What you control (examples) - Whether to accept appreciation, intervene, or stimulate domestic demand. - Whether to coordinate with others to reduce imbalances.

3.3 Pacifica (Small open financial hub)

Structural profile - Small, highly open economy specializing in finance, logistics, and high-value services.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: High
  2. Inflation: Low-Medium
  3. External balance (current account): Near balance / small surplus
  4. FX reserves: Medium-High
  5. Public debt: Low-Medium
  6. Financial depth: High
  7. Institutional credibility: Strong
  8. Trade openness: High
  9. Capital mobility: Open

Domestic politics - Finance and trade sectors demand stability and predictability. - Public is sensitive to housing/cost-of-living pressures.

Main constraints - Limited domestic policy autonomy (high exposure to global flows). - Vulnerable to sudden stops and reputational shocks.

What you control (examples) - Whether to prioritize stability via tight policy/managed FX vs. flexibility. - Whether to impose targeted capital measures during stress.

3.4 Agrona (Commodity exporter)

Structural profile - Resource- and agriculture-heavy exporter with volatile terms of trade.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: Low-Medium
  2. Inflation: Medium
  3. External balance (current account): Volatile (can swing)
  4. FX reserves: Low-Medium
  5. Public debt: Medium
  6. Financial depth: Medium
  7. Institutional credibility: Moderate
  8. Trade openness: Medium
  9. Capital mobility: Managed

Domestic politics - Rural/commodity interests are powerful. - Public pressure rises quickly during price busts and currency weakness.

Main constraints - Commodity price shocks dominate the cycle. - Limited buffers mean policy mistakes are punished quickly.

What you control (examples) - Whether to save windfalls (buffers) or spend for short-run relief. - Whether to let the currency absorb shocks or resist volatility.

3.5 Industralia (Industrializing importer)

Structural profile - Mid-income economy trying to climb the value chain; imports key capital goods and energy.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: Medium
  2. Inflation: Medium-High
  3. External balance (current account): Deficit (High)
  4. FX reserves: Low-Medium
  5. Public debt: Medium-High
  6. Financial depth: Medium
  7. Institutional credibility: Moderate
  8. Trade openness: Medium
  9. Capital mobility: Managed

Domestic politics - Industrial firms lobby for protection and credit. - Labor is organized; unemployment is politically sensitive.

Main constraints - Balance-of-payments pressure is persistent. - Credibility is earned through consistency (and lost quickly).

What you control (examples) - Whether to prioritize stabilization (tighten) vs. growth (ease). - Whether to liberalize trade/capital gradually or double down on protection.

3.6 Frontieria (Fragile, aid-dependent state)

Structural profile - Low-income economy with weak institutions; relies on aid/remittances and a narrow export base.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: Low
  2. Inflation: High
  3. External balance (current account): Deficit (Medium-High)
  4. FX reserves: Low
  5. Public debt: Medium
  6. Financial depth: Low
  7. Institutional credibility: Weak
  8. Trade openness: Medium
  9. Capital mobility: Restricted

Domestic politics - High political volatility; legitimacy is contested. - Households are highly sensitive to food/fuel prices.

Main constraints - Small shocks can become crises. - External financing often comes with conditions.

What you control (examples) - Whether to seek external support early vs. preserve sovereignty. - Whether to stabilize prices via tight policy vs. risk unrest.

3.7 Mercatoria (Trade-dependent manufacturing economy)

Structural profile - Export-oriented manufacturer integrated into global supply chains; competitiveness is central.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: Low-Medium
  2. Inflation: Medium
  3. External balance (current account): Near balance / small deficit
  4. FX reserves: Medium
  5. Public debt: Low-Medium
  6. Financial depth: Medium
  7. Institutional credibility: Moderate
  8. Trade openness: High
  9. Capital mobility: Managed-Open

Domestic politics - Exporters push for a competitive currency and stable trade rules. - Consumers dislike inflation; policymakers fear capital flight.

Main constraints - Sensitive to global demand and trade disruptions. - Thin margins mean exchange-rate swings matter.

What you control (examples) - Whether to prioritize competitiveness (weaker currency) vs. price stability. - Whether to pursue trade deals, diversification, or protection.

3.8 Petrovia (Resource-rich, politically exposed)

Structural profile - Energy/resource exporter with concentrated revenue base and elevated geopolitical risk.

Starting snapshot (tiers):

  1. Wage level: Medium
  2. Inflation: Medium
  3. External balance (current account): Surplus (Medium) but volatile
  4. FX reserves: Medium-High
  5. Public debt: Low-Medium
  6. Financial depth: Medium
  7. Institutional credibility: Moderate
  8. Trade openness: Medium
  9. Capital mobility: Managed

Domestic politics - Resource sector dominates; governance disputes are common. - Policy debates center on how to use resource revenue (save vs spend).

Main constraints - Sanctions and political risk can disrupt finance and trade. - Commodity price swings transmit quickly to the budget.

What you control (examples) - Whether to build buffers (stabilization funds) and credibility. - Whether to diversify the economy or rely on resource rents.
